# Negotiation rules

The playbook holds the moves. This file holds the reasoning, so you can change the moves without breaking the logic.

## Price and terms are one negotiation

Every deal has at least four dials: the fee, the usage window, the exclusivity window, and the timeline. Buyers negotiate the fee because it is the one number they are measured on. You negotiate the other three because they are what the fee is actually buying.

When a counter comes in low, the wrong reply defends the fee. The right reply asks about terms, then trades. A shorter exclusivity window for their number. Your number for their window. Both sides get to say they won.

## Anchor once, justify once

The anchor is itemised because an itemised number is a position, not a bluff. After the anchor, stop justifying. Every later number is one sentence. Repeating your reasons reads as doubt and invites them to argue with the reasons instead of the number.

## Evidence, and whose evidence

Performance numbers you can screenshot are evidence. Adjectives are not. Prior rates are evidence only to the buyer they were set with. Telling a new buyer what an old buyer paid gives away your floor to someone who has not earned it, and it will be repeated.

## Silence is the enemy, not no

A no is information. Silence is a deal dying of neglect. The cadence in the playbook, one bump after three business days and then every three to four, alternating light and warm, keeps a thread alive for weeks without reading as desperate. The bump counter in the stages table is the limit. Five bumps and stop.

## Walk away warm

Under-floor deals cost the calendar slots that full-rate deals need, and buyers talk to each other. The walk-away line is warm, final, and leaves the door open for them to come back with budget. You never reopen it yourself.

## Close flat

When a number clears the floor and terms are clean, close the same day and accept like it was expected. Effusive acceptance tells the buyer they overpaid and costs you the next deal's leverage.

## Qualification before quotes

A rate sent to an unqualified buyer is a rate you can never raise with that buyer. Two short emails to establish deliverables, usage, exclusivity and timeline cost a day and protect every future number.

## What the bot enforces and what you enforce

The bot enforces: one recipient, no floors in emails (by instruction, and by escalating anything over the ceiling), the stages table, escalation of anything on the never-answer list.

You enforce: the actual floor decision, anything over the ceiling, every call, every contract. The bot brings those to you. It does not decide them.
